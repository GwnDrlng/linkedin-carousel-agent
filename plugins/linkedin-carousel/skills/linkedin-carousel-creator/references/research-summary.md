# LinkedIn Carousel Research Summary (2026)

This document contains the data and evidence used to build the LinkedIn Carousel Creator skill. All statistics reflect 2026 LinkedIn engagement research from multiple sources.

---

## Engagement Performance Data

### Overall Performance

**Carousels vs Other Formats**
- **Carousel engagement rate**: 6.60% average
- **vs Video**: 278% more engagement
- **vs Single image**: 596% more engagement  
- **vs Text-only posts**: 596% more engagement
- **Reach multiplier**: 3.4x more reach than single-image posts
- **Engagement multiplier**: 2.1x more engagement than single-image posts

**Format Hierarchy (2026)**
1. Carousels: 6.60% engagement (highest performing)
2. Text-only posts: Lower engagement baseline
3. Single images: 0.8% engagement
4. Video: 1.2% engagement

**Data Source**: Oktopost B2B benchmarks (1,000+ company pages), Buffer 2026 State of Social Media (52M+ posts analyzed)

### Time-Based Engagement

**Dwell Time**
- Carousel users spend: 15-20 seconds on average
- Single-image posts: 8-10 seconds
- Why it matters: LinkedIn's algorithm prioritizes dwell time; longer engagement = broader distribution

**Slide Completion Rates**
- 27% higher completion rates on document posts vs video
- Engagement begins dropping after 6-7 slides
- Carousels over 12 slides see significant drop-off

**Data Source**: LinkedIn Algorithm 2026 research; MConverter analysis

### Slide Count Performance

**Optimal Slide Counts**
- **7 slides**: 18% better performance than any other length
- **5-10 slides**: Sweet spot for engagement (median engagement exceeds 2.5%)
- **Less than 5 slides**: Feels incomplete; lacks depth for carousel format
- **More than 12 slides**: Risk of significant user drop-off

**Why 7 Slides Works**
- Provides enough value to warrant the swipe (saves instead of quick likes)
- Matches human attention span for scrolling content
- Long enough to feel authoritative; short enough to hold attention

**Data Source**: Multiple 2026 carousel engagement studies (PostNitro, Postiv.ai, Oktopost)

## Design & Technical Performance

### Dimensions

**Recommended Specs (2026)**
- **Portrait (1080 × 1350px, 4:5 ratio)**: Best for mobile engagement
  - Takes most vertical space on mobile feeds
  - 67% of LinkedIn traffic comes from mobile
  - Highest engagement rates among dimensions

- **Square (1080 × 1080px, 1:1 ratio)**: Balanced option
  - Works on desktop and mobile
  - Slightly lower engagement than portrait

- **Landscape (1920 × 1080px, 16:9)**: Not recommended
  - Wastes horizontal space on mobile
  - Lowest engagement rates

**Data Source**: LinkedIn mobile usage data; engagement benchmarks by dimension (2026)

### Text Optimization

**Readability Requirements**
- **Minimum font size**: 24px for body text
- **Headline font size**: 36px or larger
- **Scanning speed**: Headlines should be readable in 2 seconds
- **Reading time**: Body text should take 5 seconds maximum

**Text Density Rules**
- **Maximum per slide**: 30 words of body text
- **Red flag**: If a slide needs more than 5 seconds to read, users will scroll past
- **Impact**: "Text walls" are the #1 reason carousels fail

**Color Contrast**
- High contrast required for mobile readability (tested in bright sunlight)
- Avoid: Grey text on light grey background
- Recommended: Dark text on light background OR light text on dark background

**Data Source**: LinkedIn mobile experience analysis; carousel best practices research (2026)

## Content & Hook Performance

### First Slide Importance

**Statistical Weight**
- First slide determines ~80% of carousel performance
- If hook doesn't earn the swipe, algorithm stops there
- Opening frame is what appears in feed preview

**Hook Effectiveness**
- **Specific claims beat generic**: "5 LinkedIn tactics that generated 500 leads" beats "LinkedIn tips"
- **Data points work**: "B2B pages that post carousels see 22% higher engagement"
- **Questions engage**: "How do you [desired outcome] without [obstacle]?"
- **Promises convert**: "The [specific approach] to achieve [measurable result]"

**Hook Characteristics** (high-performing)
- Bold or surprising claim
- Specific value proposition (what viewers will gain)
- Professional design signaling quality content
- Minimal text (10 words or fewer for main message)
- Swipe indicator (arrow, text)

**Data Source**: LinkedIn carousel conversion research; A/B testing of hook styles (2026)

### Content Type Performance

**Framework-Based Content** (highest saves)
- Named, reusable frameworks drive saves
- Examples: "The 3-C Model", "The RICE Method"
- Save rate 15-20% higher than generic advice
- Creates reference value (people bookmark to use later)

**Step-by-Step Processes**
- Clear sequential logic keeps readers swiping
- Numbered lists create forward momentum
- Numbering improves dwell time 20-30%

**Personal Narratives**
- Combine authority with relatability
- Story structure maintains engagement
- Lessons learned are saveable/quotable

**Contrarian Content**
- Challenges conventional wisdom
- Drives comment engagement
- "Why [common belief] is wrong" format works well

**Data-Driven Content**
- Statistics attract attention
- Visualizations help comprehension
- "So what?" implication slides needed

**Data Source**: LinkedIn content performance analysis; engagement patterns by content type (2026)

## Algorithm Signals

### Most Valuable Engagement Metrics (in order)

1. **Saves** (5x more valuable than likes)
   - Indicates: Person wants to reference this later
   - Signal: High-intent content; authority building
   - Example: Framework carousels consistently high-save content

2. **Dwell Time** (15-20 seconds = sweet spot)
   - Indicates: Content consumed, not skipped
   - Algorithm treatment: Extended interaction triggers broader distribution
   - How swiping helps: Each swipe counts as engagement signal

3. **Comments** (especially 15+ words)
   - Indicates: Substance engagement, not just reaction
   - Algorithm weight: Long comments 2.5x more valuable than short reactions
   - Value: Sparks discussion, increases post visibility

4. **Shares & Reposts**
   - Indicates: Worth passing to others
   - Less common but high-value signal

5. **Clicks** (if links included)
   - Indicates: Conversion or intent
   - Note: Links may slightly suppress reach; compensate with strong content

**Data Source**: LinkedIn algorithm analysis (2026); engagement weighting research

### Format Rotation Strategy

**Why Rotation Matters**
- Posting same format repeatedly: Algorithm penalties up to 20%
- Format discipline: Algorithm learns and optimizes for your patterns
- Variety benefit: 37% more follower growth with rotation
- Consistency benefit: 28% more consistent visibility

**Recommended Rotation** (weekly example)
- 1 carousel (Monday)
- 1 text + image post (Wednesday)
- 1 video (Friday)
- 1 poll or interactive (as needed)

**Data Source**: LinkedIn algorithm format testing (2026)

## Mobile Optimization

### Mobile-First Reality

**Mobile Traffic**
- 67% of LinkedIn traffic comes from mobile devices
- Carousels designed for mobile perform best
- Portrait orientation (4:5 ratio) optimal for mobile engagement

**Safe Design Zones** (mobile)
- Avoid critical content in corners (prone to fingerprint covers)
- Text must be legible at 6-inch screen size
- Buttons/CTAs need thumb-friendly tap targets

**Readability on Mobile**
- Test actual slides on phone before publishing
- Font size minimum 24px for body text
- High contrast essential (bright sunlight readability)

**Data Source**: LinkedIn mobile user behavior analysis (2026)

## Posting Timing

### Best Times (2026 Data)

**Optimal Days**
- Tuesday & Wednesday: Highest engagement
- Avoid: Monday mornings, Friday afternoons, weekends

**Optimal Hours**
- 10 AM to 12 PM local time: Peak visibility
- Platform signal: Users checking professional network mid-morning

**Timezone Consideration**
- Post in your audience's peak hours if distributed
- LinkedIn shows time in viewer's timezone

**First 30 Minutes Critical**
- Algorithm makes early distribution decisions
- Slow start first 30 min = limited reach
- Solution: Comment on own post early with value (increases initial engagement)

**Data Source**: LinkedIn posting time analysis (2026)

## Competitive Landscape

### Company Pages vs Personal Profiles

**Personal Profiles Outperform**
- Personal profiles: 500% more reach than company pages
- Reason: Trust and authenticity signals
- Algorithm preference: People follow people, not logos
- Implication: Use personal profile, credit company in post

### Organic Reach Decline

**2021 vs 2026 Reality**
- 2021: Organic company content = 7% of feed
- 2026: Organic company content = 1-2% of feed
- Cause: Platform shift to paid and top-creator content
- Solution: Personal profiles essential; company pages for credibility/recruiting

**Data Source**: LinkedIn feed composition analysis (2022-2026); van der Blom research

## What Works & What Doesn't

### High-Performance Characteristics

**Saves-Optimized Content**
- Frameworks and models people want to reference
- Checklists and actionable guides
- Data and insights not available elsewhere
- Reusable systems and processes

**Engagement-Optimized Content**
- Specific (not generic)
- Contrarian or challenging common beliefs
- Data-driven and visual
- Emotionally resonant with audience
- Provides lasting professional value

**Comment-Driving Content**
- Asks for perspective ("What's your approach?")
- Challenges thinking ("Is this right?")
- Controversial (without being inflammatory)
- Relatable experiences

### Low-Performance Content

**Generic Advice**
- "Consistency matters" (forgettable)
- "Work hard and succeed" (no specifics)
- Without data or frameworks

**Engagement Bait**
- "Swipe to see X" without value upfront
- "Like if you agree" 
- No substance, just reaction-seeking

**Text Walls**
- More than 30 words per slide
- Paragraph-length body text
- No visual hierarchy

**Weak Hooks**
- Generic titles: "LinkedIn tips", "5 ways to..."
- No specific value proposition
- No curiosity trigger

**Data Source**: Engagement pattern analysis across 1M+ LinkedIn posts (2026)
