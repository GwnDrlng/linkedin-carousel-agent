# LinkedIn Carousel Creator: Quick Reference

## 60-Second Summary

1. **Plan**: Define hook, choose narrative arc (framework, step-by-step, narrative, etc.)
2. **Outline**: 5-10 slides, one idea per slide, 30 words max per slide
3. **Design**: 1080×1350px, 2 fonts, 3 colors, high contrast, mobile-first
4. **Export**: PDF Standard, check text size (24px+ body, 36px+ headlines)
5. **Publish**: LinkedIn post > Document > Upload PDF > Add caption > Post
6. **Monitor**: Track saves (most important), dwell time (15-20 sec target), comments

## The 5-Minute Outline Template

```
SLIDE 1: HOOK (Most important)
- One bold claim, stat, or question
- 10 words or fewer
- Includes swipe indicator (→)

SLIDES 2-6: VALUE (Core content)
- One headline per slide
- 2-4 lines body text
- Structured arc (Problem → Solution → Action)

SLIDE 7: CALL-TO-ACTION (Clear next step)
- Save this? Comment? Follow?
- One specific action
```

## Hook Formulas That Work

- **Challenge**: "Why [common belief] is actually holding you back"
- **Question**: "How do you [solve problem] without [obvious solution]?"
- **Promise**: "The [specific thing] I use to [measurable result]"
- **Stat**: "[Number]% of [group] miss this [important practice]"
- **Contradiction**: "Everyone says you need X. We found Y works better"

## Caption Formula

"[Hook about topic]. Swipe for [what they'll get] 👇"

Example: "I see founders making 5 mistakes with their GTM. Swipe through to see which ones are costing you revenue 👇"

**Rules:**
- 3-4 lines max
- Lead with value, not mystery
- One clear CTA (swipe, comment, save)

## Posting Workflow (LinkedIn)

1. Click "Start a post"
2. Click + icon → "Document"
3. Upload PDF file
4. Add document title (clear & specific)
5. Write caption (see formula above)
6. Click "Post"
7. Monitor first 30 minutes for engagement

## Timing

- **Best**: Tuesday-Wednesday, 10 AM-12 PM local time
- **Avoid**: Friday afternoon, weekends, Monday morning

## Red Flags (Content)

- Generic titles: "LinkedIn Tips" → Rewrite: "5 Tactics That Generated 500 Leads"
- Weak hook: Doesn't promise value → Rewrite with specific outcome
- Text walls: More than 30 words per slide → Cut ruthlessly
- No CTA: Ends with "thanks for reading" → Replace with specific action
- Too long: More than 12 slides → Delete low-value slides
- Inconsistent design: Random fonts/colors → Apply brand kit consistently

## Narrative Arc Options

1. **Problem → Insight → Example → Action** (educational)
2. **Numbered Framework** ("5-Step Process")
3. **Step-by-Step Tutorial** ("How to...")
4. **Before/After** (transformation)
5. **Personal Narrative** (story + lesson)
6. **Data + Implication** (stat-driven)
7. **Contrarian Argument** (challenge common wisdom)

## Top 3 Reasons Carousels Fail

1. **Weak hook** (slide 1 doesn't earn the swipe)
   → Fix: Use specific claim, stat, or question

2. **Text walls** (too much content per slide)
   → Fix: 30 words max, split into multiple slides

3. **No clear structure** (reads like scattered thoughts)
   → Fix: Choose one arc (framework, step-by-step, narrative) and stick to it

## Top 3 Reasons Carousels Succeed

1. **Specific frameworks** (people save things they can reuse)
   → Name it: "The 3-C Model for X", "The RICE Framework"

2. **Clear progression** (each slide creates curiosity for the next)
   → Use numbered lists, problem-insight-action arcs

3. **Saves-driven content** (treats reader's time as precious)
   → Educational value > entertainment; referenceable > one-off observations
